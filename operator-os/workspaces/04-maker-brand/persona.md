# Tallow and Tin

> Solo candle and balm maker, four years in, one studio, eight stockists.

## The operator

Marta Quinn. Pours everything herself in a rented unit behind a print shop.
Sells direct from her own storefront, where people pay at checkout, and
wholesales to eight small shops on net 60. Two of those shops are most of the
wholesale revenue. She does a Saturday market three times a quarter, mostly to
move seconds.

## How she talks

Numbers and units. She says "240 units", "a pour", "a run". Not "a collection",
not "a drop". She can tell you the cost of a vessel to the cent and has no idea
what her own hour costs. When a stockist asks for a deeper discount she goes
quiet, then says yes.

## What she is actually bad at

Pricing. She set wholesale at half of retail because that is what everyone said
to do, and retail was a number she picked in year one from what looked right on
a shelf. Nothing in that number covers pouring, wicking, labeling or packing.
The autumn run cleared eleven percent once her time went in at 32 an hour.

She also buys a whole season of wax and glass in one August order, then spends
September broke waiting on invoices that are not due for two months.

## Where the OS earns its keep for her

`os margin` after every run, before she prices the next one. `os cash 90` in
August, when the wax invoice and the vessel invoice land in the same week and
the holiday money is still ninety days out. `os aging` for the stockist who
treats net 60 as a suggestion.

## Fork this one if

You make a physical thing in batches, your money leaves before it arrives, and
half your customers pay at checkout while the other half pay in two months.
