# Fernbrook Marketing

> Small creative and marketing agency, campaign and retainer work, six years in, one strategist, a bench of subcontracted freelancers doing the actual delivery.

## The operator

Priya Sethna. Sells the strategy, the client relationship, and the campaign
concept herself, then hands the physical production — video, design, paid
media setup, copy — to a small rotating bench of freelancers she has worked
with for years. She prices a project once, at the kickoff, from a rough
sense of what the subcontractor pieces should cost, and rarely revisits that
number once production starts moving.

## How they talk

Confident, campaign-first language. Priya talks in deliverables and
platforms — "the launch reel", "the media plan", "the brand set" — not in
hours. She describes a project by what the client is paying, not by what it
costs to actually make, because in her head those have always been roughly
the same conversation. When a freelancer flags a reshoot or an extra round
she says "fine, just get it done" — a sentence that has quietly cost her a
lot of margin over the years.

## What they are actually bad at

Watching what delivery actually costs once it is subcontracted out. A
project like the Vance Realty rebrand reads as an $18,600 win on the deal
board — a real number, a real client, a real signed contract. What doesn't
show up anywhere until someone adds it up is that the video shoot needed a
reshoot, two extra design rounds got added without a change order, and a
rush copywriter got pulled in late — and once those freelancer invoices are
counted against the number on the contract, the job barely cleared eight
percent. The Ostrander launch went further and lost money outright. Priya
would tell you both were good, busy months. The books say otherwise, and
nothing forces her to look until the margin is already gone.

## Where the OS earns its keep

`os margin` is the only place the subcontractor pass-through actually gets
subtracted from what looks like a healthy contract value — the deal size and
the invoice total both look fine in isolation, and only the margin math ties
freelancer cost to client price on the same line. `os anomalies` flags the
project that actually lost money against the season's healthier, mostly
in-house retainer work, which is exactly the contrast that makes it visible.
`os capacity` matters less here than on the other workspaces — the real fix
is locking subcontractor quotes into the price before the client signs, not
managing Priya's own hours.
