# Second Desk Operations

> Solo fractional ops consultant, three retainers, one client paying more than half the bills.

## The operator

Nora Halstead. Nine years running operations inside other people's companies,
four years running her own. She sells months, not deliverables. Three retainers
plus one project is the ceiling and she is sitting on it. Northgate Logistics
pays 9,000 of the 16,200 that comes in every month. She knows that number. She
has known it since about month four.

## How she talks

In scope and out of scope, said out loud, in the meeting. She quotes hours back
at people. "That is six hours and month three has four left in it." Every
proposal she writes has a last day on it. She never says partner, journey or
alignment. When a client asks for one more thing she says "yes, in month four"
and writes it down where they can see it.

## What she is actually bad at

Starting the timer. Month eleven at Northgate shows eight hours logged against a
thirty four hour retainer, which is not what happened. The work happened in
messages at 11pm and never reached a time entry. She also lets scope widen
inside a month rather than reopen the contract, because reopening it feels like
asking for something. Cotter Fabrication bought a Friday review and now gets a
daily call.

## Where the OS earns its keep for her

`os margin` per retainer before she renews anything. That is the report that
shows Cotter returning 14 percent against a 62 percent target while Northgate
returns 77. Then `os brief` for the concentration and the renewal date, and
`os capacity` on a Friday so the hours she owes are a number instead of a
feeling. She does not need `os chase`. Her clients pay. She needs to know which
month she is in and what she agreed to do in it.

## Fork this one if

You bill the same amount every month whatever happens, your clients ask for
things that were never in the scope, and one name on your invoice list is worth
more than all the others put together.
