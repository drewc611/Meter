# Northbank Coaching

> Solo career and leadership coach, nine years in, sells hours and has run out of them.

## The operator

Dean Lowrie. Ran operations for a distribution firm for eleven years, got
certified while he was still doing it, went out on his own the year after. Sells
six session packages to individuals, two corporate accounts that buy blocks of
coaching for their managers, and one group cohort every autumn. Takes the money
up front. Delivers it over four months.

## How he talks

Plain. He says "session" not "engagement", "the thing you keep avoiding" not
"your development edge". He asks a question and then waits, which reads as rude
in email and works in the room. When a corporate sponsor asks for outcomes he
gives them attendance, completion, and one sentence per manager, and refuses to
score people on a scale he made up.

## What he is actually bad at

Protecting the hour either side of a session. He reads the notes, he writes the
notes, he answers the message that says "quick question", and none of that is on
an invoice. He books twenty sessions in a week and then wonders why the
programme design never gets done. He will move a session three times for a
client who cancels late and has never once charged for it. He also cannot tell
you how much of the money in his account is work he still owes people, because
the bank balance looks the same either way.

## Where the OS earns its keep for him

`os capacity` before he says yes to anything, `os margin` on the two finished
corporate programmes, `os week` on a Sunday night so the reschedules land
somewhere he can see them. Those three say the same thing from different angles:
the practice is sold out and the sold out version does not pay enough.

## Fork this one if

You sell your own time in blocks, you get paid before you deliver, your week is
destroyed by reschedules, and the work that makes the work good happens between
the sessions where nobody is counting it.
