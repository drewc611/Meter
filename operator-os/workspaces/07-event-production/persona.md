# Harlow Bay Events

> Full-service event production, weddings and corporate, nine years in, a coordinator, a bench of vendors on call.

## The operator

Jess Calloway. Runs every event from first venue walk to load-out, backed by
one part-time coordinator and a rotating bench of caterers, florists, AV and
rental vendors she has used for years and trusts to invoice her fast. Every
event runs on the same shape: a deposit at signing, vendor deposits go out as
soon as each one is booked, and a balance invoice goes to the client a couple
of weeks before the event. Corporate clients route that balance through an
approval chain Jess has no visibility into and no leverage over.

## How they talk

Direct, vendor-and-date focused. Jess says "the balance is out" not "we
billed them", "their AP" not "the client", "load-in" not "the event day."
When a corporate client's payment is slow she does not call it a collections
problem, because it isn't one — she says "that's just how long Calder takes",
flatly, the way you'd describe weather. She never chases a corporate invoice
early; she has learned it does nothing.

## What they are actually bad at

Seeing the gap between when the money goes out and when it comes back in.
Every vendor deposit is paid the moment a vendor is booked — venue, catering,
florals, AV — often ten and twelve weeks before the event. The client's
balance, especially a corporate one, doesn't land until well after that,
sometimes six or seven weeks after the invoice goes out, regardless of the
fifteen-day terms printed on it. None of this shows up as a slow-paying
client in the aging sense — Calder & Wills always pays in full, eventually.
It shows up as the business fronting five figures of vendor cash for months
at a stretch, on multiple events running at once, with nothing forcing
anyone to look at the calendar gap between the two.

## Where the OS earns its keep

`os cash 90` is the whole point here, not `os aging` — aging would tell Jess
Calder pays fine, which is true and useless. The cash timeline is the only
place a vendor deposit paid last month and a client balance not expected for
another six weeks sit on the same line, and it is the only place the dip
between them shows up before it becomes a real problem paying a vendor on
time. `os brief` surfaces the balance-invoice follow-ups so the reminder
still goes out even though Jess knows chasing rarely moves the date.

## Fork this one if

You pay vendors out before the client's own money has cleared, the client
isn't the risk — they pay every time — and the danger is purely the gap in
the calendar between when your cash goes out and when it comes back.
