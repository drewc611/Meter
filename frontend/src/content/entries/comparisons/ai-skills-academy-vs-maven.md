[Maven](https://maven.com/) is a real, working platform with a large catalog: a marketplace for cohort-based courses, where independent instructors set their own price and schedule and Maven takes a platform cut (around 10%) on top. It offers three tiers -- free "Lightning Lessons" (30-45 minute workshops), paid half-day-to-one-day workshops (roughly $500), and full cohort courses that run $950-$3,500+ depending on the instructor and depth, with the average course landing around $500. Courses run synchronously on a fixed schedule with cohort discussion and direct instructor access for the run's duration, then the cohort ends.

## What Maven gets right

Maven's real strength is selection and instructor quality: hundreds of courses across every subject, taught by people who are often genuine practitioners and operators rather than generalist course creators, with the accountability and energy that a live, scheduled cohort with a hard start and end date actually produces. If you want to learn from one specific named expert, on a fixed multi-week cadence, with classmates moving through the same material at the same time, that's a real and different experience than a self-serve archive -- and Maven does that well, at scale, across a huge range of topics well beyond AI.

The tradeoff is the one built into that model: price and schedule are set per-instructor and per-course, so what you pay and when the next cohort starts vary course to course, and a course you want may not be running right now. There's also no persistent, always-on membership tier across Maven itself -- each course is its own purchase, its own calendar, its own commitment.

## Where AI Skills Academy differs

AI Skills Academy isn't trying to be a marketplace -- it's one continuously available track (the 30-day challenge on governed agentic AI engineering, adapted from our own handbook), with the first few days free to read with no signup, and a single flat monthly membership price for the rest, live workshops, and courses as they ship. There's no per-course purchase decision and no waiting for a cohort's start date -- the material is there whenever you start, and membership is one price rather than a $500-3,000 per-course commitment.

The honest caveat: that focus is a real, current limitation, not just a design choice presented as one. Maven has hundreds of instructors and topics; AI Skills Academy has one subject area and one instructor's material, with the live-workshop and course tracks both still in production rather than live today. If what you want is choice of instructor and topic, or the accountability of a scheduled cohort with a hard deadline, Maven's model is genuinely better suited to that. If what you want is one focused track you can start today for free and pay a flat, predictable price to go deeper on, that's the gap this exists to fill.
