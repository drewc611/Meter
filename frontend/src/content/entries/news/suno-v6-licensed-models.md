Suno retired its previous model lineup on September 9, 2026 and replaced it entirely with v6, v6-wild, and v6-mini -- its first models trained on officially licensed catalogs from Warner Music Group, BMG, and Believe, with revenue-sharing for rights holders built into the product from the start rather than added after the fact. New capabilities include prompt-based song editing, image- and video-referenced generation, and instrument separation.

## Licensing as the actual news, not the feature list

Suno Chief Product Officer Jack Brody framed the company's position on content governance directly: it's "up to the distributors and the platforms to govern what content goes on there." That's a generative-AI company drawing a line between what it trains on (now licensed) and what gets uploaded to it (still the platform's call) -- a distinction that matters more than any single new editing feature, given how many of Suno's copyright lawsuits turned on training data rather than output moderation.

## What this signals for enterprise buyers of generative media

A major consumer generative-AI product moving from unlicensed scraping to a revenue-share licensing model, mid-lawsuit, is a concrete governance and liability data point -- not just for music specifically, but for any company evaluating generative-media tools where the underlying training-data provenance is the actual legal exposure, not just the output quality.
