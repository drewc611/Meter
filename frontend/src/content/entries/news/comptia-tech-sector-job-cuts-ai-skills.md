CompTIA data reported September 9, 2026 shows the tech sector shed roughly 14,700 workers in August 2026, even as other sectors added a combined 86,000 tech-titled roles. Tech leads 2026's layoffs overall, with more than 155,000 cuts so far this year -- 29% of all announced layoffs across every sector. IT-specific unemployment rose to 3.1% from 2.8%. In the same month, job postings requiring AI skills reached 320,000, up 4.5% from July.

## Two numbers moving in opposite directions, in the same data set

Glassdoor Chief Economist Daniel Zhao's read on the pattern: "the tech sector is still generally shedding jobs." That the sector doing the most cutting is simultaneously the one seeing AI-skill demand climb isn't a contradiction so much as a restructuring signal -- the roles being cut and the roles being posted aren't necessarily the same roles.

## What this means for workforce planning around AI adoption

For any company modeling its own AI-driven workforce changes, this data separates two things that get conflated in headlines: overall tech headcount contraction, and specific demand for AI skills within whatever headcount remains. Both are real and both are accelerating at the same time -- which is a more complicated planning problem than either trend read in isolation.
