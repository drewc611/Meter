Governor Newsom signed AB 1709 (Asm. Josh Lowenthal) on September 10, 2026, as part of the same 13-bill child-safety package that included Adam's Law and the AI-generated-CSAM statute already covered on this site. AB 1709 bans addictive engagement features -- autoplay and algorithmic content feeds specifically -- for users under 16, and passed the legislature with no recorded opposition. A companion bill in the package, AB 2, separately raises the civil damages large platforms owe when their products harm minors.

## A different target than the rest of the package

Where SB 1119 and SB 1276 focus on companion chatbots and AI-generated abuse imagery, AB 1709 targets the recommendation-engine mechanics behind any AI-personalized feed -- the same algorithmic ranking systems that power short-video apps, social platforms, and increasingly AI-driven content discovery generally. OpenAI's own public comment on the broader package, via Chris Lehane, was that it "meets the moment for parents and sets teens up for educational success" -- notable as a frontier AI lab publicly endorsing engagement-limiting regulation rather than opposing it.

## Why this reaches beyond social media specifically

Any product using an AI recommendation or ranking system to personalize content for users who could be under 16 -- not just social apps in the traditional sense -- now has a concrete California compliance requirement to check its feed logic against. Passing with zero recorded opposition is itself a signal: this is the kind of engagement-mechanics regulation with little political cost to support, which makes it a reasonable bet for other states to copy quickly.
