Google has started testing direct in-chat purchases from Flipkart -- the Walmart-owned Indian e-commerce platform -- through Gemini and Google's AI Mode, TechCrunch reported September 26. Select users see a "Buy" button on qualifying Flipkart product listings that routes straight into a Flipkart checkout flow without leaving the AI interface. The pilot currently covers a narrow set of categories: smartphones, electronics, and mobile accessories.

## A visible asymmetry

Amazon listings appear in the same Gemini and AI Mode results but carry no equivalent purchase option -- a pointed detail, given Google has invested $350 million in Flipkart and built a "Universal Commerce Protocol" explicitly meant to let AI interfaces transact across retailers generally, not just one partner. Whether that protocol stays retailer-neutral in practice, once a paying partner is in the room, is exactly the kind of platform-power question this test surfaces before the feature has even left India.

## Timing is deliberate

Google says it plans to broaden the rollout in October, positioned ahead of India's festive shopping season -- the country's highest-volume e-commerce stretch of the year. That timing, plus the narrow initial category list, reads as a controlled test of conversion and trust in AI-native checkout before a much bigger, revenue-relevant push, rather than an instant broad launch. For anyone tracking whether "AI shopping agents" actually transact versus just recommend, this is one of the first concrete instances of a major platform wiring a real payment flow directly into a chat interface.
