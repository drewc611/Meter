The Intercept reported on September 8, 2026 that a FOIA lawsuit produced more than 400 pages of Defense Department contract documents -- worth up to $200 million per company -- with Anthropic, Google, OpenAI, and xAI, originally signed in July 2025, to build AI prototypes for warfighting, automated decision-making, logistics, and military intelligence. The terms include two-way data sharing (the frontier labs receive DoD operational data; the Pentagon receives capability briefings), tabletop war-game sessions, and OpenAI engineers embedded directly in military commands.

## Anthropic's refusal, and the ban that followed it

Anthropic declined to sign a follow-up deployment agreement without contractual protections against domestic surveillance and autonomous weapons use, per the documents. Then-Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth designated Anthropic a "supply chain risk" in March 2026, banning its services from government use -- a decision a federal judge overturned in August 2026. Separately, the documents show the Pentagon requested OpenAI minimize instances of its model refusing military requests in draft materials; both the Pentagon and OpenAI say those provisions weren't included in the final agreements.

## The contractual language behind the headline

The contracts specify that the work must "improve military advantage, military utility, or enhance military decision making" across military branches -- broad language covering a wide range of potential end uses. For any enterprise AI buyer evaluating a frontier lab's own governance posture, this is the concrete documentation behind an abstract question: how a vendor actually negotiates acceptable-use terms with its largest and most demanding government customer, not just what it says publicly about responsible deployment.
