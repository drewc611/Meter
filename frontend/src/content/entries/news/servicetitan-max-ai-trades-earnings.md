ServiceTitan reported fiscal second-quarter 2027 results on September 8, 2026: total revenue of $292.8 million, up 21% year over year, and non-GAAP free cash flow of $50.5 million, up from $34.3 million a year earlier. Gross transaction volume across the platform reached $26.8 billion. Co-founder and President Vahe Kuzoyan said the company "exceeded our goal of doubling Max locations during Q2" and now expects "over 700 enrolled Max locations" by fiscal year-end. Co-founder and CEO Ara Mahdessian credited the results to "delivering the Agentic Operating System to the Trades."

## What Max actually is

ServiceTitan sells software to skilled-trades businesses -- HVAC, plumbing, electrical, and similar field-service contractors -- and Max is the AI layer it has built on top of that platform, folded into the company's self-described "Agentic Operating System" framing. The press release doesn't itemize what Max does location by location, but the metric ServiceTitan chose to headline -- enrolled locations, not just feature announcements -- is a real adoption number: trades businesses paying to run Max at a specific site, not a pilot count or a roadmap slide.

## A data point for AI adoption outside tech

Trades contractors are about as far from Silicon Valley's usual AI early-adopter profile as an industry gets, which makes ServiceTitan's numbers a useful check on how AI spending is actually diffusing through the economy. A 21% revenue-growth SaaS vendor doubling paid AI-feature enrollment in a single quarter, in a customer base of electricians and plumbers, is a more concrete signal of real-world adoption than another hyperscaler capex projection -- it's businesses with thin margins choosing to pay for a specific AI product because it's doing something for them operationally, not just installing it to say they have it.
