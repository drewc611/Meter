Illinois governor JB Pritzker signed Executive Order 2026-07 on September 22, establishing an Illinois Artificial Intelligence Cabinet -- a standing body of state officials and outside experts in cybersecurity, data privacy, ethics, law, and technology, tasked with advising state government on AI-related risk. Pritzker must appoint the cabinet's members within 30 days of the order.

## What the cabinet actually does

The order's scope is state government's own exposure, not private-sector AI use: the cabinet advises agencies on preparing for AI-related incidents, identifying safeguards for public assets and infrastructure -- government buildings, water systems, education systems, information infrastructure -- and evaluating further steps Illinois could take to strengthen AI safety and accountability. It's an advisory body, not a regulator; it doesn't itself have enforcement power over anything in the private market.

## The second move in one summer

This follows, rather than replaces, Illinois' Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act, signed earlier this summer -- one of the more expansive state-level AI safety laws passed so far. Pritzker's office frames the cabinet as a response to continued inaction at the federal level, alongside a specific news hook: another AI-industry whistleblower resignation cited in the announcement. For any company with Illinois-based infrastructure or state contracts, the near-term relevance is limited to the cabinet's own remit -- public-sector risk -- but it's a signal of the direction state-level AI policy is headed absent a federal framework.
