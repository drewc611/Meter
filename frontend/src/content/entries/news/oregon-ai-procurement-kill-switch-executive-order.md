Oregon governor Tina Kotek signed Executive Order 26-26, "Establishing Responsible Artificial Intelligence Procurement Standards for State Government," on September 23. It directs the state's chief information officer, Terrence Woods, to develop third-party safety review standards for frontier AI models before state agencies can procure them, and to assess -- not yet require -- whether those models should come equipped with a kill switch. Woods has 90 days to submit an implementation proposal.

## An assessment, not a mandate

The headline framing in several outlets overstates where the order currently lands: it does not mandate a kill switch. It tells the CIO's office to determine whether requiring one is viable for state government's own AI purchases, alongside broader third-party safety review standards for models the state buys. Kotek's stated rationale is the familiar one this cycle -- that federal safeguards aren't in place, so states are moving on their own -- and the order includes a built-in check: Kotek will reassess whether it's still necessary every three months, and it stays in effect until she terminates it.

## Reading it against Illinois' cabinet, the same week

Oregon's order lands one day after Illinois' AI Cabinet order, and both share a shape: state government regulating its own AI purchasing and risk exposure via executive order, in the absence of a federal framework, rather than legislating private-sector AI use directly. Neither order changes what a company selling AI products into the private market has to do. The thing worth tracking is Woods' 90-day proposal -- that's where an actual kill-switch requirement, if one emerges, would first take concrete shape.
