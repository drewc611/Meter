The United Arab Emirates pledged to invest 40 billion euros (about $46.5 billion) in Germany during President Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan's state visit to Berlin on September 10, 2026, with a data center of up to 1 gigawatt of capacity named as part of the package alongside energy and industrial commitments. The visit also produced 29 separate business agreements worth 9.4 billion euros, and builds on a February 2026 memorandum between Germany's RWE and Abu Dhabi's ADNOC for a decade of LNG imports. Current bilateral trade between the two countries runs about $15 billion annually. UAE Industry Minister Sultan Ahmed Al Jaber put it plainly: "The UAE invests for the long term and builds partnerships that endure."

## Gulf capital moving from chips to the buildings that house them

Most of the AI-infrastructure money flowing out of the Gulf this year has gone into compute deals -- GPU purchases, cloud joint ventures, chip-adjacent financing. A sovereign-level pledge that names a specific gigawatt-scale data center inside a broader bilateral investment package is a different category: it's Emirati capital underwriting the physical infrastructure layer in a G7 economy directly, at head-of-state level, rather than through a corporate joint venture.

## What's still unconfirmed

Neither government has named the site, the operator, or which UAE entity will structure the data-center piece specifically. Pledges announced during state visits routinely take months or years to convert into signed, sited projects, so the number to watch next isn't the $46.5 billion topline but whatever specific data-center agreement, if any, eventually gets named.
