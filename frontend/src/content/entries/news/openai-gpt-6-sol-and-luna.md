OpenAI released two new models on September 22: GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna, both built on the technology behind GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI's frontier release earlier in September) but repackaged as faster, cheaper alternatives rather than a new flagship tier. API pricing is roughly half of GPT-5.6's promotional rate: $2/$10 per million input/output tokens for Sol, and $0.10/$0.50 for Luna.

## Two models, two jobs

Sol is aimed at demanding everyday work -- OpenAI's own framing is that it reaches "Astra-level reliability at much lower cost." Luna is aimed at high-volume tasks where per-token cost dominates the decision. OpenAI's factuality claim -- that Sol makes about half as many mistakes as its predecessor -- comes from the company's internal evaluation built on de-identified real-world conversations where users flagged errors, which is a real methodology but not an independent benchmark; treat the specific "half as many" figure as OpenAI's own claim rather than externally verified.

## Availability

Both models are live now for Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise, and Edu users in ChatGPT Work and Codex, with API access enabled at launch; free and Go-tier users get GPT-6 Luna in the desktop app. Combined with Anthropic's Opus 5.5 price cut the same day, the pattern this week is the same on both sides: frontier labs pushing near-flagship quality down into their cheaper tiers, rather than only pushing the top tier's capability up.
