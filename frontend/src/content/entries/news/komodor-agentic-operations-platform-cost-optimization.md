Komodor, the AI-SRE vendor backed by $90 million in venture funding and used inside Fortune 500 shops including Nebius, Dell, Cisco and Prudential, launched its Agentic Operations Platform on September 16. The pitch combines more than 50 pre-built "specialist" agents spanning AI SRE, AI software operations and cost optimization with a shared backbone -- persistent memory, context management, cost controls, security guardrails and audit trails -- that lets a platform team either run those agents as-is or build and govern custom ones on the same infrastructure. Co-founder and CTO Itiel Shwartz framed the actual bottleneck bluntly: "The hardest part of running agentic operations in production is not building the agents. It is keeping every agent grounded in the right context."

## Governance, not capability, is what Gartner says kills these projects

The launch leans on a specific, citable data point: Gartner's projection that more than 40% of agentic AI initiatives will be decommissioned by 2027 over governance gaps rather than the agents simply not working. That's a sharper claim than the usual "AI project failure rate" statistic, because it locates the failure downstream of a successful pilot -- an agent that works in a demo but has no persistent memory, no cost ceiling and no audit trail is the one that gets shut off once it's in front of production traffic and a CFO.

## Why it matters

This is one vendor's press release, not independent research, so its own adoption numbers should be read that way. But the shape of the product says something true about where enterprise AI spend is actually getting stuck this year: the constraint has shifted from "can an agent do the task" to "can anyone tell what it's costing, whether it's still doing the right thing six months in, and who signed off on it." A cost-optimization agent that itself needs a cost ceiling and an audit trail is a fairly honest admission that agentic tooling has become its own line item to track, not just a way to cut existing ones.
