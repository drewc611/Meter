Senators Amy Klobuchar (D-MN), Ted Cruz (R-TX), and Majority Leader John Thune are working on bipartisan federal legislation aimed at guardrails against catastrophic AI risk -- biological and nuclear misuse specifically -- according to reporting on September 10, 2026. Bill text isn't public yet; introduction was expected as soon as the following week. This is a commitment to introduce legislation, not an introduced bill.

## Why the pairing matters more than the bill itself, so far

Cruz and Klobuchar have publicly disagreed on AI regulation's basic shape for years -- Cruz generally skeptical of federal rules, Klobuchar generally in favor of them. Sen. Klobuchar's own description of the goal: "legislation for commonsense guardrails to address the risks posed by AI systems." A bill with both of their names on it, plus the Majority Leader's direct involvement, is a different signal than the usual party-line AI proposals that go nowhere.

## What enterprise compliance teams should actually watch for

OpenAI's Chris Lehane has already weighed in publicly, urging lawmakers to prioritize real action over a perfectly drafted bill -- industry lobbying before text even exists. Since nothing is filed yet, there's nothing to comply with today, but a genuine bipartisan push on catastrophic-risk federal rules is the kind of development that can move fast once text lands, and it's worth having on a compliance team's radar well before a floor vote.
