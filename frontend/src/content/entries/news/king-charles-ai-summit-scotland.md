King Charles III hosted a summit of AI industry executives at Dumfries House in Scotland on September 17, bringing together representatives from OpenAI, Anthropic, Nvidia, Google DeepMind and the UK's AI minister, NPR reported. "We are witnessing one of the greatest technological leaps in the history of human endeavor," the king told attendees, adding "there is a clear imperative to ensure that this rapidly evolving technology remains safe and secure." It's a return to a role he first took on in 2023, when he opened the UK's inaugural AI safety summit.

## Discussion, not decisions

NPR's reporting describes a summit built around fostering conversation among industry leaders who hold genuinely different views on AI safety -- Gina Neff, deputy CEO of Responsible AI UK, framed its purpose that way -- rather than one structured to produce a joint statement, pledge or policy outcome. No agreements or commitments came out of the meeting, per the report. The event also drew protest: activists left large anti-AI slogans on nearby beaches as executives arrived, an echo of the same organizers who've campaigned against new data centers outside the Scottish Parliament.

## A symbolic venue, a substantive week

Coming the same week OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind confirmed they'd been quietly coordinating on AI safety for weeks, and days after Google DeepMind launched its own AGI-focused DeepMind Institute, the royal convening reads more as atmosphere than substance -- a head of state lending visibility to a safety conversation the labs are already having with or without him. For anyone tracking whether AI governance is heading toward binding rules or just more forums, this is squarely the latter: no reader evaluating AI vendor risk gets anything actionable out of a summit that produced a quote and no commitments.
