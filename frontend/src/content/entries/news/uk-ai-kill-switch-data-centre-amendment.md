An amendment to the UK's Cyber Security and Resilience Bill, currently at House of Lords committee stage, would let the Secretary of State order emergency shutdown of AI systems or data centres judged to pose a "catastrophic risk" to national security, critical infrastructure, or public safety -- and would require data-centre operators to pre-install the infrastructure needed to comply with such an order before one is ever issued. It was tabled by Liberal Democrat peer Lord Tim Clement-Jones, with cross-party co-sponsors: Conservative peer Baroness Dido Harding, crossbencher Baroness Beeban Kidron, and Labour's Lord Philip Hunt -- one of 65 amendments debated during the week of September 1, 2026.

## Cross-party sponsorship, not a fringe proposal

Four sponsors spanning three parties plus a crossbencher is a meaningfully different signal than a single backbencher's amendment -- it suggests the idea has real support across the political spectrum, even if it doesn't survive committee in its current form. Civo CEO Mark Boost's reaction captures the debate's other half: "it only solves half the problem regarding our national security," pointing to Britain's broader dependency on foreign AI infrastructure as the issue a kill switch alone doesn't fix.

## What a mandatory kill switch would actually mean operationally

A binding statutory shutdown power, paired with mandatory pre-installed kill-switch infrastructure at data centres, would be a novel compliance and operational-continuity requirement for any enterprise running AI infrastructure or workloads in the UK -- worth flagging to infrastructure and compliance teams now, at bill stage, rather than after it potentially becomes law.
