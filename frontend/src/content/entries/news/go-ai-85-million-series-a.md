Go.AI, a Chicago-based provider of on-premises AI infrastructure for banks, healthcare providers, and other regulated organizations, raised an $85 million Series A led by Updata Partners, with existing investors GFT Ventures and LAUNCH participating. The round brings the company's total funding to $90 million.

## The product is the hardware, not just the model

Go.AI's core offering, The Go1, is described as on-prem, examiner-ready AI hardware and software built specifically so regulated institutions can run AI without sending data to a third-party cloud -- a direct answer to the compliance objection that stalls a lot of AI adoption inside banks and healthcare systems. The company rebranded from Go Abacus earlier this year alongside The Go1's launch.

## Growth numbers, with the usual caveat

Go.AI reports more than 200 customers, annual recurring revenue up more than eightfold year-over-year, and says the business is already profitable. Those are the company's own disclosed figures rather than independently audited ones -- standard for an early-stage funding announcement, but worth noting as such. The capital is earmarked for engineering headcount, product development, and expanding go-to-market beyond regulated industries specifically, which is the more interesting signal here: a company built for compliance-heavy verticals betting there's a broader on-prem AI market to sell into next.
