Cohere and Aleph Alpha signed a definitive business combination agreement on September 16, formalizing a deal the two companies first disclosed back in April. The combined business will operate globally under the Cohere name, dual-headquartered in Toronto and Berlin, with Aleph Alpha's Heidelberg site kept on as a dedicated research center and a combined headcount above 1,000. SiliconANGLE reports the deal reportedly values the merged company around $20 billion, up sharply from Cohere's own $7 billion valuation a year earlier; neither company's own announcement discloses a figure.

## Why "sovereign" is the whole pitch

Cohere co-founder and CEO Aidan Gomez framed the combination around a specific customer anxiety, not a capability gap: "No government or enterprise should have to choose between capable AI and control over their technology." Aleph Alpha built its business largely around European government and regulated-industry customers who need models they can run on-premises or in domestically controlled infrastructure, rather than call out to a US-hosted API. Aleph Alpha co-CEO Ilhan Scheer, who becomes the combined company's COO, put it as bringing "what our team has built to more organizations worldwide." The deal also folds in a STACKIT integration with German retailer Schwarz Group's sovereign cloud, and per SiliconANGLE's reporting, Schwarz has separately committed roughly €500 million in financing alongside a potential $3 billion from a Canadian government-backed consortium.

## What it means for buyers already tracking AI spend

For a company deciding where to route enterprise AI workloads, this is a bet that "which model" is no longer just a capability-and-price question -- it's also a jurisdiction question, especially for public-sector and regulated customers who can't have their data or their model weights subject to a foreign company's terms of service. Whether the combined Cohere can actually deliver frontier-competitive quality on top of that sovereignty pitch, rather than trading capability for control, is the thing worth watching once the deal closes later this year and enterprise contracts start testing the claim against real workloads.
