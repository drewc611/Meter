Yelp announced on September 10, 2026 that it is integrating OpenAI's newly released GPT-Live-1 voice model into two products built for small businesses: Yelp Host, which answers restaurant phone calls to take reservations and orders, and Hatch, its AI communication platform for service businesses like contractors and salons. Yelp Host has handled more than 1 million calls since its October 2025 launch; Hatch has managed tens of millions of leads across voice, text, email and web. Yelp Chief Product Officer Akhil Kuduvalli Ramesh said the tool's value "is not only how it sounds, but how deeply it understands each restaurant's operations." Hatch co-founder and CEO Chris Bache said "great voice AI requires more than a great voice model," pointing to years spent on how service businesses actually operate. OpenAI's Teri Yu, its multimodal product lead, said it's "exciting to see customers like Yelp and Hatch use these capabilities to solve everyday problems for local businesses."

## What the new model changes

GPT-Live-1's headline feature is full-duplex audio -- the system can listen while it's speaking, so a caller can interrupt, correct an order mid-sentence, or talk over background noise without breaking the conversation, rather than the turn-taking, wait-for-a-pause interaction older voice assistants require. Yelp says production testing already shows improved call-handling and fewer transfers to a human, though it hasn't published a specific before-and-after number for either metric.

## Enterprise AI adoption, one small business at a time

Neither restaurants nor HVAC contractors are the customers usually pictured in enterprise-AI spending stories dominated by bank technology budgets and hyperscaler capex, but Yelp's million-call figure is a real usage number from a product that's been live for almost a year, not a pilot announcement. The pattern -- a platform vendor absorbing the newest foundation-model capability and re-selling it as an operational tool to small operators who'd never buy or build voice AI themselves -- is likely to be a bigger channel for actual AI diffusion into the economy than any single enterprise signing its own contract with a model provider.
