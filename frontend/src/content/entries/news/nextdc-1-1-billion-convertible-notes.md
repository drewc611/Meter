NEXTDC priced an A$1.1 billion (roughly $795 million) convertible notes offering on September 10, 2026, maturing in September 2031 with a 1.75% coupon and a conversion price of $16.695 per share -- a 32.5% premium to the $12.60 reference price. Net proceeds after related capped-call transactions are expected to be about $1.006 billion, which NEXTDC says will fund its Australian data-center development pipeline. The company also entered capped call transactions with a $21.42 cap price to limit dilution, and plans to list the notes on the Vienna Multilateral Trading Facility rather than the ASX. Pro forma available liquidity stood at roughly $9.8 billion as of June 30, 2026.

## The third raise since early 2026

This is NEXTDC's third capital markets trip in four months, following an A$1.8 billion senior debt facility and a $750 million subordinated notes offering earlier in the year. CEO Craig Scroggie said the deal "provides NEXTDC with efficient, committed funding for our development pipeline and diversifies NEXTDC's sources of capital." Three raises in that short a window is itself the signal: AI-ready data-center capacity is capital-intensive enough that even a well-funded incumbent operator needs to keep returning to the market rather than financing growth off a single large facility.

## What it's actually funding

NEXTDC hasn't disclosed a specific new capex figure tied to this raise, but the company has previously flagged sharply rising capital expenditure to meet AI-driven demand for contracted, power-ready capacity. For anyone tracking how much real construction sits behind data-center announcements, a repeated cadence of convertible-note and debt raises -- rather than one large headline figure -- is a more reliable signal of sustained build-out than a single funding announcement.
