The Pentagon's Office of Strategic Capital is negotiating a roughly $5 billion loan to Fluidstack, an AI cloud-computing startup, according to Wall Street Journal reporting carried on the Reuters wire on September 10, 2026. The loan is structured to fund manufacturing and supply-chain capacity for data-center components -- shoring up the broader US supply chain that feeds AI infrastructure buildouts -- rather than financing a specific new facility.

## A government office built for exactly this

The Office of Strategic Capital exists specifically to make this kind of investment: government capital underwriting the industrial base an emerging technology depends on, the same logic historically applied to semiconductors and critical minerals now extended directly to AI data-center supply chains. A loan at this scale, to a company most outside the industry haven't heard of, says more about how seriously the Pentagon takes AI infrastructure supply risk than a headline chip-export policy would.

## Why the supply-chain framing matters

Most AI infrastructure financing stories are about compute capacity directly -- gigawatts, chips, data centers. This one is upstream of that: the components and manufacturing capacity that data-center buildouts depend on. For anyone modeling how exposed the broader AI infrastructure buildout is to supply bottlenecks, a government loan aimed specifically at that layer is worth tracking as its own category, separate from compute-capacity announcements.
