OpenAI said on September 6, 2026 that it has reached a goal CEO Sam Altman first floated in an October 2025 livestream: fielding an "automated research intern" by September 2026, with a further goal of a "legitimate AI researcher" by March 2028. In a blog post covering what it calls research acceleration, the company said its research organization is now logging 3.1 "agent-workdays" of effort for every workday put in by a human researcher -- and that the system, per Engadget's Jackson Chen, is "a system that can carry out well-defined research tasks under human direction" -- work that would otherwise take a skilled researcher several days.

## What "intern-level" actually means here

OpenAI is explicit that this isn't an autonomous scientist setting its own research agenda -- it's a supervised agentic system executing a bounded objective a human hands it, then returning the work for evaluation. The company's own numbers show the limits of that supervision: on tasks estimated to take 4 to 8 hours, more than half of successful attempts over the past six months still involved at least one human intervention along the way, according to Unite.AI's independent reporting on the same announcement. That's a real capability jump from a year ago, and also a long way from research running itself.

## The spend behind the milestone

The infrastructure cost of getting here is substantial and, per OpenAI's own disclosure, uneven across its research staff: by mid-August 2026, the median researcher was running more than $600 a day of inference at API prices, with the heaviest users past $7,000 a day. That's a real operating cost most companies don't carry, and it's worth naming plainly -- this milestone was reached by a lab spending enormous sums on its own product to accelerate its own research, not by a breakthrough in efficiency.

## Grading your own homework

Every figure in this announcement -- the 3.1x ratio, the intervention rate, the inference spend -- comes from OpenAI measuring OpenAI's own internal research process against a target OpenAI itself set a year earlier, with no independent benchmark or outside auditor in the loop. That doesn't make the claim false, but it does mean the milestone should be read as a lab's own progress report rather than a third-party verification that the "automated research intern" bar has actually been cleared. The company's own hedge is worth taking at face value too: it frames these as preliminary measurements, not a settled result.
