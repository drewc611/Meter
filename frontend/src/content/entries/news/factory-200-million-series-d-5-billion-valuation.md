Factory, the company behind the "Droid" coding agent, announced on September 15 that it has raised $200 million at a $5 billion valuation, according to its own blog post. That's more than triple the $1.5 billion valuation the company carried after its $150 million Series C round just five months ago, in April, and brings Factory's total funding past $400 million. The round was backed by Blackstone, Khosla Ventures, Sequoia Capital, Insight Partners, Evantic Capital, Sound Ventures, NEA, Mantis VC and Clearlake, with angel checks from Nico Rosberg, Brad Gerstner and Marc Benioff.

## What Factory actually sells

Factory was founded in 2023 by Matan Grinberg and Eno Reyes, and its pitch is autonomous agents ("Droids") that handle software tasks beyond writing code -- refactors, migrations and deployments -- deployable in the cloud, on-premises or fully air-gapped. Since Factory 2.0 shipped in April, the company says it has added Factory Router, which assigns models per task and claims to cut token spend by more than 60%, plus an "Agent Effectiveness" feature aimed at letting enterprises measure the actual value of their AI spend rather than just usage volume. Its blog describes the goal as giving enterprises "control over how their software factory learns, which models it uses, and where it runs."

## Context: a fast-compounding category

The jump is one data point in a broader repricing of coding-agent startups this year -- Cursor's maker was acquired by SpaceX for $60 billion in August, and rival Cognition raised $2 billion at a $48 billion valuation on September 9, tripling its own valuation in four months. Whether that pace of repricing reflects proportional enterprise adoption, or is running ahead of it, is exactly the kind of question a spend/value tracker is built to answer once these tools show up in seat counts and usage logs rather than funding announcements.
